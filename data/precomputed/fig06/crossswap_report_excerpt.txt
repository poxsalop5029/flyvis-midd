# report16_17_integrated.md — Integrated Phase 16–17 Report

## From Common-Mechanism Search to Context-Dependent Seed Structure in FlyVis MIDD Flow

**Status:** Phase 16 complete; Phase 17 complete  
**Model population:** existing official pretrained FlyVis ensemble, with Phase 17 restricted to `flow/0000/000 ... 049`  
**Independent confirmation:** not performed  
**Final integrated closure:** `distributed_coadapted_context_dependent_MIDD_without_stable_seed_taxonomy`

---

# 1. Integrated executive summary

Phases 16 and 17 addressed one continuous mechanistic question:

> **Why can a static MIDD stimulus produce illusion-like decoded rotational Flow in FlyVis, and is that phenomenon generated by one common mechanism or by different mechanisms across trained models?**

Phase 16 attacked the problem from the **location / necessity** side. It tested upstream nonlinear dynamics, distributed representation, decoder readout, network–decoder co-adaptation, recurrent state, localized intervention, and sampling geometry.

Phase 16 did **not** identify a single common MIDD mechanism. Several simple universal explanations were weakened:

- upstream local nonlinearity was not selectively necessary;
- 048 and 207 did not share a fixed canonical linear population representation;
- localized decoder-input populations selected from spatial correlation were not selectively necessary against matched controls;
- recurrence affected the system but not in a MIDD-specific way;
- sampling/rendering altered the phenotype but did not provide a stable trained-seed explanation.

The strongest positive Phase 16 result was instead **network×decoder co-adaptation**. Cross-swapping network and decoder identities showed interaction-dominated variance for both shared bias `S` and antisymmetric component `A`.

Because no fixed common mechanism emerged, Phase 16 ended with:

```text
H4 = best_remaining_leaning_not_proven
primary_closure = mixed_coadapted_multiple_realizability_like
```

Phase 17 then directly tested that unresolved H4 possibility.

Using the official pretrained 50 seeds only, Phase 17 built a 74-feature mechanism-only fingerprint without using MIDD labels or baseline output phenotype for clustering. The resulting mechanism space was highly structured but almost one-dimensional. A multi-component GMM existed, but the discrete solution contained a singleton and therefore failed the predeclared stable-regime criterion.

Most importantly, once this mechanism structure was frozen:

- frozen clusters did not associate with MIDD occurrence;
- the dominant mechanism axis did not associate with MIDD occurrence;
- the complete mechanism fingerprint did not predict MIDD in nested CV;
- same-seed fingerprints were closer across 048↔207 than random pairings, but detailed rank and fingerprint organization were not strongly preserved.

Thus Phase 17 **did not validate the strong form of multiple realizability** in which each seed occupies a stable alternative mechanistic pathway that determines MIDD.

The integrated conclusion is therefore:

> **MIDD Flow is not explained by a single fixed common mechanism among the tested candidates, but it is also not explained by a stable seed-specific taxonomy of alternative MIDD mechanisms. The evidence instead supports a distributed, network–decoder co-adapted computation whose internal expression depends on both trained seed and stimulus context.**

This picture is compatible with MIDD Flow being an emergent error/byproduct-like output of a generic motion-processing system rather than the product of a dedicated illusion-specific circuit, but that interpretation remains a computational working model rather than a demonstrated biological claim.

# 2. Core output decomposition

Throughout Phase 16–17, decoded Original and Mirror rotational tangents were decomposed as:

\[
A=\frac{T_O-T_M}{2},
\]

\[
S=\frac{T_O+T_M}{2},
\]

and:

\[
M=|A|-|S|.
\]

Therefore:

\[
T_O=S+A,
\]

\[
T_M=S-A.
\]

Actual reversal occurs when:

\[
T_O T_M<0,
\]

equivalently:

\[
|A|>|S|,
\]

or:

\[
M>0.
\]

This decomposition separated two conceptually different contributors:

- `A`: stimulus-antisymmetric Original–Mirror component;
- `S`: shared seed/model rotation bias.

A central result of Phase 16 was that both quantities depend strongly on the trained network–decoder pair.

---

# 3. Initial mechanistic hypotheses

Phase 16 formalized four broad hypotheses.

## H1 — Upstream nonlinear residual

A nonlinear recurrent transformation creates a non-cancelling Original–Mirror difference before decoding.

## H2 — Distributed representation × decoder readout

The mechanism is distributed across spatial activity and decoder geometry rather than localized to one cell or stage.

## H3 — State / sampling / shared bias

Initial state, recurrence, receptor sampling, rendering geometry, or shared bias `S` drives the apparent reversal.

## H4 — Seed-specific multiple pathways

Different trained seeds realize the MIDD phenotype through different internal mechanisms.

The project explicitly required more than heterogeneity to accept H4.

---

# 4. Phase 16 — Mechanistic narrowing

## 4.1 Stage emergence: upstream information exists, but is stimulus-dependent

For 3MIDD 048, seed-wise decoded `A` was predictably represented in intermediate network stages:

- medulla CV R² ≈ **0.352**
- transmedullary CV R² ≈ **0.290**
- all-network CV R² ≈ **0.325**

Medulla was the earliest internal candidate.

For 207, the same relation was weak.

Therefore:

> Upstream spatial information exists, especially for 048, but no universal earliest MIDD stage was established.

This supported distributed upstream structure while immediately weakening a single stimulus-invariant pathway.

---

## 4.2 2×2 upstream / decoder linearization: simple nonlinearity is not selectively necessary

The FF / LF / FL / LL replay separated upstream and decoder nonlinear dependence.

Neither 048 nor 207 showed the selective MIDD impairment expected if one localized nonlinear stage were the common generator.

Formal Phase 16 interpretation:

```text
no_selective_nonlinearity_evidence
```

Therefore H1 was weakened.

---

## 4.3 Canonicalized 048↔207 transfer: no fixed shared linear population code

Sign canonicalization failed to reveal a stage/family whose linear representation transferred between 048 and 207.

Final branch:

```text
fixed_common_linear_representation_unsupported
```

This did not exclude nonlinear or context-dependent common computation, but it weakened a fixed shared H2 representation.

---

## 4.4 Network×decoder cross-swap: strongest positive Phase 16 result

A 10×10 network×decoder cross-swap decomposed variance into:

- network identity;
- decoder identity;
- interaction.

For `S`, interaction fractions were approximately:

| Stimulus | Interaction fraction |
|---|---:|
| 3MIDD 048 | **0.734** |
| 3MIDD 207 | **0.725** |
| 4MIDD | **0.730** |

For `A`, interaction fractions were approximately:

| Stimulus | Interaction fraction |
|---|---:|
| 3MIDD 048 | **0.726** |
| 3MIDD 207 | **0.677** |
| 4MIDD | **0.675** |

After motion-gain normalization, `S` interaction fractions were about **0.805** for all three MIDD conditions.

Thus:

> Neither network identity nor decoder identity alone explains the bias structure. The trained network–decoder pairing matters strongly.

This was the clearest evidence for co-adapted distributed computation.

---

## 4.5 Recurrent state: important to computation, not uniquely MIDD-specific

Gray-state reset nearly eliminated MIDD `A`, but normal-motion output was impaired even more strongly.

MIDD-adapted operating points retained much of `A`:

- 048 `r_A_adapt ≈ 0.859`
- 207 `r_A_adapt ≈ 0.823`

and adapted-reset states retained roughly:

- 048 `r_A_adaptreset ≈ 0.796`
- 207 `r_A_adaptreset ≈ 0.810`

Thus recurrence is important to the network, but a special MIDD-only accumulation process was not supported.

---

## 4.6 Localized decoder-input intervention: no selective causal population

Cross-fitted target populations were selected from spatial activity–`A` relations and compared against matched controls.

For 048:

```text
target-minus-matched A loss median = -0.00341
95% CI = [-0.00662, -0.00157]
```

For 207:

```text
median = -0.000288
95% CI = [-0.001489, +0.000155]
```

MIDD-minus-motion selectivity was positive, opposite the desired MIDD-selective impairment.

Final branch:

```text
localized_readout_selectivity_not_supported
```

This weakened a localized H2 explanation.

---

## 4.7 Sampling / resolution: pair-level effect without stable seed replication

A 2-3-2 / rendering bridge improved some pair-level metrics, but the trained-seed global RMSE improvement confidence interval crossed zero.

Final classification:

```text
sampling_geometry_not_supported
```

Sampling mattered, but was not elevated to the common mechanism.

---

# 5. Phase 16 closure

Phase 16 concluded:

```text
single_common_mechanism_identified = False

H1 = weakened
H2 = mixed_common_mechanism_weakened
H3 = partial_mixed
H4 = best_remaining_leaning_not_proven

primary_closure =
    mixed_coadapted_multiple_realizability_like
```

The mechanistic picture after Phase 16 was:

```text
Static MIDD
   |
   +--> upstream spatial O–M information
   |        +--> strong in 048
   |        +--> weak / non-transferable in 207
   |
   +--> distributed recurrent computation
   |
   +--> network × decoder co-adaptation
   |        +--> strong effect on A
   |        +--> strong effect on S
   |
   +--> state / sampling modulation
   |        +--> real
   |        +--> not common MIDD-specific explanation
   |
   +--> decoded output
            T_O = S + A
            T_M = S - A
```

At this point H4 was the best remaining explanation because several common mechanisms had failed.

But it had not been directly demonstrated.

---

# 6. Why Phase 17 was necessary

There is an important logical distinction:

> Failure to find one common mechanism does **not** prove multiple realizability.

At least three possibilities remained after Phase 16:

1. stable seed-specific alternative mechanisms;
2. a continuous model-to-model axis rather than discrete pathways;
3. context-dependent computation in which a seed's internal mechanism changes with stimulus.

Phase 17 was designed to distinguish these possibilities.

# 8. Phase 17 unsupervised result — structure exists, but not a stable taxonomy

After block-wise robust scaling and equal block weighting:

```text
PCA components required = 1
explained variance = 99.9569%
```

Thus the measured mechanism fingerprint was dominated by a nearly one-dimensional axis.

GMM:

```text
BIC best k = 5
subsampling k>1 fraction = 1.0
median ARI = 0.869
minimum cluster size = 1
```

The singleton component violated the predeclared minimum cluster size of five.

Formal branch:

```text
weak_or_unstable_discrete_regime
```

Therefore:

> The mechanism space is structured, but `k=5` is not evidence for five validated mechanistic pathways.

The safer model is a dominant continuous axis plus weaker local mixture structure.

Once the unsupervised structure was frozen, MIDD labels were opened.


No association.

## Frozen dominant PC1

```text

```

No association.

## Full mechanism fingerprint

Nested-CV logistic prediction:


```text
mechanistic_structure_not_reliably_predictive_of_MIDD
```

Therefore the central Phase 17 negative finding is:

> **The measured seed-level mechanism phenotype does not define which official seeds exhibit MIDD reversal.**

# 10. Cross-stimulus stability — seed identity exists, but is context dependent

048 was the discovery stimulus and 207 was the frozen replication stimulus.

Same-seed 048↔207 fingerprints were significantly closer than random seed pairings:

```text
pairing permutation p ≈ 1.0×10^-4
```

This is real evidence that trained seed identity matters.

However:

```text
PC1 Spearman = 0.085
median full-fingerprint Pearson = 0.459
median full-fingerprint cosine = 0.489
```

Only one of three predeclared strong seed-stability criteria passed.

Secondary cluster metrics:

```text
same-cluster fraction = 0.86
ARI = 0.126
```

Formal branch:

```text
mixed_seed_and_stimulus_dependence
```

The appropriate interpretation is:

> **Seeds are not interchangeable, but their internal mechanistic expression is strongly stimulus dependent.**

---

# 11. The unusual PC1 Pearson / Spearman result

Phase 17 observed:

```text
PC1 Pearson  = 0.999
PC1 Spearman = 0.085
```

This difference is too large to summarize as a generic “high correlation.”

The analysis plan had predeclared Spearman rank preservation as the stability criterion, and that criterion failed.

Therefore the integrated interpretation retains both facts:

- there is strong linear-scale correspondence in the projected PC1 values;
- seed ordering is not preserved.

This unusual divergence should be displayed transparently if used in a paper, ideally with the raw 048-vs-207 PC1 scatter and seed labels.

It does not overturn the final mixed seed×stimulus conclusion.

# 13. Integrated answers to the major mechanistic questions

| Question | Integrated answer |
|---|---|
| **Is there one common MIDD mechanism?** | No common mechanism was identified among the tested upstream, decoder, state, localized, and sampling hypotheses. |
| **Is medulla the generator?** | No. Medulla is an early 048 candidate representation stage, not a proven generator, and the relation is weak in 207. |
| **Is upstream nonlinearity necessary?** | Not supported as a common selective requirement. |
| **Is the decoder alone responsible?** | No. Network×decoder interaction is strong; decoder-only explanations are insufficient. |
| **Is there a localized causal decoder-input population?** | Not supported by the matched-control intervention. |
| **Is recurrence uniquely required for MIDD?** | No. Recurrence is important to normal motion processing as well, and adapted states retain MIDD `A`. |
| **Is BoxEye sampling the main cause?** | Not supported as the stable trained-seed mechanism. |
| **Do 048 and 207 use one fixed representation?** | Not supported. |
| **Do different seeds use stable alternative MIDD mechanisms?** | Not supported by Phase 17. |
| **Does seed identity matter at all?** | Yes. Same-seed cross-stimulus fingerprints are closer than random pairing. |
| **Is seed identity expressed identically across stimuli?** | No. The mechanistic organization is substantially stimulus dependent. |
| **Can the measured mechanism fingerprint predict MIDD?** | No, not reliably in nested CV. |

# 14. Current mechanistic picture

The combined evidence supports the following conservative computational picture:

```text
Static MIDD stimulus
        |
        v
receptor sampling / rendered input
        |
        v
distributed recurrent network computation
        |
        +--> seed-dependent internal state
        |
        +--> stimulus-dependent population geometry
        |
        v
network × decoder co-adapted readout
        |
        +--> antisymmetric component A
        |
        +--> shared component S
        |
        v
decoded Original / Mirror Flow
        |
        +--> reversal when |A| > |S|
```

Important consequences:

### 1. No dedicated single location has been identified

There is no evidence that one stage, cell class, decoder nonlinearity, state variable, or sampling transformation universally generates MIDD Flow.

### 2. Co-adaptation is real

The network and decoder cannot be treated as independent modules for mechanistic interpretation.

### 3. Model variation is real but dynamic

Different trained seeds are not identical, yet their mechanistic differences are not fixed pathway labels.

### 4. MIDD occurrence is an output phenotype, not a stable seed class

A seed's measured mechanism fingerprint does not reliably tell whether it will satisfy the MIDD reversal criterion.

### 5. Context matters

The internal organization expressed by the same model changes substantially between 048 and 207.

---

# 15. What the combined study ruled out or substantially weakened

The combined Phase 16–17 program weakened the following strong claims:

1. **Simple upstream nonlinear residual is the universal MIDD mechanism.**
2. **One fixed 048↔207 population representation explains the effect.**
3. **A localized decoder-input population is selectively necessary.**
4. **A special MIDD-only recurrence process is required.**
5. **Sampling geometry alone explains the trained-seed phenotype.**
6. **The official 50 seeds divide into stable MIDD-relevant mechanism classes.**
7. **A dominant continuous seed mechanism axis predicts MIDD occurrence.**
8. **Stable seed-specific multiple realizability explains why some seeds show MIDD and others do not.**

These negative results substantially narrow the mechanistic search space.

---

# 16. What remains positively supported

The combined program positively supports:

- structured upstream O–M information, particularly in 048;
- strong network×decoder co-adaptation;
- large interaction contributions to both `A` and `S`;
- real state dependence of the motion system;
- real sampling/rendering modulation;
- substantial output phenotype heterogeneity;
- substantial mechanism-space structure;
- residual trained-seed computational identity;
- significant same-seed similarity across stimuli relative to random pairings;
- substantial stimulus dependence of internal mechanistic expression.

These results are compatible with a distributed emergent computation.

---

# 17. Revised interpretation of “multiple realizability”

The term **multiple realizability** can refer to several different claims.

## Strong version

> Distinct trained seeds belong to stable alternative internal pathways, and those pathway classes generate the same MIDD phenotype.

**Not supported.**

Phase 17 did not find stable MIDD-linked pathway classes.

## Weak version

> The same general architecture can express different internal configurations across trained seeds and stimuli while producing related decoded behaviors.

**Compatible with the data.**

However, because these configurations were not stably linked to MIDD occurrence, the integrated report prefers the less loaded term:

> **context-dependent heterogeneity**

for the current evidence.

---

# 18. Relationship to the “computational error / failure mode” hypothesis

A motivating hypothesis for the broader project is:

> A static image sequence should ideally decode as static; illusion-like Flow may reflect a computational failure mode of a generic motion system.

The Phase 16–17 findings are compatible with this idea because:

- no dedicated MIDD circuit was identified;
- the effect depends on distributed network–decoder computation;
- model and stimulus context strongly modulate the output;
- the same model architecture can produce different internal organizations across stimuli.

However, the present analyses do **not** prove that MIDD Flow is an optimization error or establish why learning produces it.

The appropriate current language is:

> **MIDD Flow is consistent with an emergent byproduct / failure-mode-like output of generic motion computation, but the learning-dynamics origin remains a separate hypothesis.**

A future study of training trajectory, sparsity, intermediate checkpoints, or architectural efficiency should therefore be framed as a new phase rather than appended to Phase 16 or 17.

---

# 19. Limitations of the combined Phase 16–17 evidence

## 19.1 Internal validation only

The same pretrained ensemble contributed to hypothesis development and testing.

No newly trained independent seed population was used.

## 19.2 Negative evidence is mechanism-class specific

Failure of a fixed linear representation does not rule out every nonlinear common transformation.

Failure of localized intervention does not rule out redundant distributed causality.

Failure of the 74-feature taxonomy does not prove that every possible seed-level feature is unrelated to MIDD.

## 19.3 Decoder-input intervention is not recurrent-circuit intervention

Phase 16 localized intervention tested causal contribution at decoder input, not causal generation inside the recurrent network.

## 19.4 Compact full-50 decoder challenge

Phase 17 used native plus five fixed reference decoders rather than all 2,500 network×decoder combinations.

## 19.5 PCA / GMM interpretation

The near-one-dimensional Phase 17 fingerprint was derived from the chosen A–E descriptors and weighting scheme.

The discrete GMM solution failed a minimum-size criterion and should not be reported as five biological/mechanistic types.

## 19.6 PC1 cross-stimulus discrepancy

The Pearson≈0.999 / Spearman≈0.085 divergence is unusual.

The final decision correctly followed the predeclared Spearman-based rank criterion, but any manuscript use should show the raw scatter and inspect leverage rather than citing Pearson alone.

# 21. Implications for the paper / next research phase

The strongest paper story is not:

> “We found the MIDD mechanism.”

Nor is it:

> “We proved multiple realizability.”

The stronger evidence-based story is:

> **Systematic mechanistic dissection progressively eliminated several simple explanations for illusion-like decoded motion. The remaining computation is distributed and co-adapted, while model-to-model variation is real but stimulus dependent and does not define a stable MIDD class.**

This provides a useful computational result because it shows why reduction to:

- one cell type;
- one stage;
- one decoder bias;
- one recurrent state;
- one sampling artifact;
- or one stable model subtype

fails.

A new phase, if pursued, should answer a different question rather than further subdividing the same seed taxonomy. Candidate directions include:

- training-dynamics origin of illusion Flow;
- intermediate checkpoints;
- sparsity / computational-efficiency trade-offs;
- architecture perturbation;
- new independently trained seeds for confirmatory testing.

These should be pre-specified as a new research phase.

# 23. One-paragraph integrated paper-style conclusion

Using an existing ensemble of trained FlyVis motion models, Phases 16 and 17 systematically tested whether static MIDD-induced decoded Flow could be attributed to a common upstream nonlinearity, a fixed distributed representation, decoder readout geometry, recurrent state, localized decoder-input populations, sampling geometry, or stable seed-specific alternative mechanisms. Phase 16 identified stimulus-dependent upstream spatial information and strong network–decoder co-adaptation, but selective linearization, canonical cross-stimulus transfer, localized matched-control intervention, state-reset specificity, and sampling-based explanations failed to establish a universal MIDD mechanism. Phase 17 then constructed a 74-feature mechanism-only fingerprint across the 50 official pretrained models. Although the mechanism space was highly structured and nearly one-dimensional, its discrete mixture solution was not sufficiently stable to define a robust taxonomy, and neither frozen clusters, the dominant continuous axis, nor the full fingerprint predicted MIDD occurrence in held-out seeds. Same-seed fingerprints remained significantly closer across 048 and 207 than random seed pairings, demonstrating residual model identity, but detailed mechanistic organization was strongly stimulus dependent. Together, the results favor a distributed, network–decoder co-adapted, context-dependent computation and argue against both a single fixed MIDD mechanism and a stable seed-specific mechanism taxonomy as explanations for the illusion-like Flow phenotype.
